# Memphis — Product core hypothesis

## Promise

Understand what is happening around a song, what is happening inside it, and how it connects to your musical world.

This sharpens the vision without declaring the product validated. Preserve the existing frontend and Design Archive; use the song and artist interfaces as foundations for exploration.

## A repeatable song experience

1. **What changed?** Show a dated attention timeline for the sources we actually cover. Separate sustained growth, a short spike and renewed attention to an older recording.
2. **What might explain it?** Place sourced events beside the timeline: a release, performance, screen appearance or other documented exposure. Explain that timing suggests a possible connection, not proof of cause.
3. **What can I hear?** Offer a short listening guide and, where reviewed information exists, observations about structure, rhythm, arrangement or vocal delivery.
4. **What connects?** Surface credited collaborators, related works and specific musical connections with reasons.
5. **What next?** Let the listener compare, save a question, follow a collaborator or explore another work.

Standardize these questions and the evidence behind each answer. Do not require identical biographies, complete coverage or a long essay for every song. Missing information remains missing.

## Useful analysis candidates

| Question | Method to explore | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is this growing or already large? | Compare recent activity with the same recording's earlier activity, showing absolute change and relative growth. | Small baselines exaggerate percentages; cumulative totals alone do not show current momentum. |
| Is this a comeback? | Compare renewed activity with release dates and prior observations. | Release dates can refer to reissues; recordings and versions must be matched. |
| Did attention last? | Follow a spike through later observation periods. | Results describe only the observed source and period. |
| Who connects my favorites? | Match explicitly credited roles across saved or voluntarily supplied listening records. | Incomplete credits limit coverage; a shared credit does not prove shared sound. |
| What sounds similar across different artists? | Compare reviewed annotations such as arrangement, rhythmic feel or vocal technique. | Similarity depends on the chosen dimension and may be interpretive. |

Do not combine views, listeners and chart positions into an unexplained popularity score. A chart position is not a stream count. Always retain source, timestamp, coverage and method; annotate changes in source measurement definitions.

## Why return?

A listener follows songs, people or musical questions. Memphis brings back a meaningful development: renewed attention to a saved song, newly documented context, another work by a followed collaborator, or a comparison related to an interest.

The proposed loop is: **discover → understand → save or follow → receive a relevant development → explore again.**

Start with a useful weekly experience. A daily visit is not required. Test whether people voluntarily return and explore an explained connection; visits and notifications alone do not establish value.

## Positioning

Last.fm already offers listening statistics, reports and discovery. Chartmetric already offers professional track analytics and chart monitoring. Memphis should not claim to have invented music tracking or to provide analysis nobody else offers.

The proposed distinction is a listener-facing combination of changing context, musical explanation and personal connections. It must earn its place by making those connections easier to understand and act on than separate searches.

## First experiment

Select roughly ten songs with enough evidence to support one complete journey. Include an older song receiving renewed attention, a recent release and works connected by collaborators. Reuse the existing song page design for a timeline, one listening observation, a sourced connection and a comparison.

First verify one usable trend data source, its access cost, update frequency and permissions for storage and display. Do not promise live or historical tracking before that check. Mock timelines must be labeled as demos.

Keep sound annotations editorial at first. Automated deep audio analysis is a separate capability requiring suitable audio access and validation. Credits and popularity data cannot establish a song's musical structure by themselves.

Ask a few curious listeners to explore, then return the following week with meaningful updates. Test whether they learned something, understood a comparison and wanted to follow it further before expanding the catalog or building advanced personalization.
